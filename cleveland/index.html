Keeping Your Cleveland HVAC System Running Longer

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still cheap.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than scrambling for a new system during the worst heat wave of summer.

The earlier you report a comfort problem, the cheaper it usually is. A weak capacitor is an easy, low-cost part to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected through a full season, can take out the compressor and force a full system replacement. Save our number on your phone now so help is one call away the moment something sounds or feels wrong in your home. Early intervention saves time, money, and miserable nights.
