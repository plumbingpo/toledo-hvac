How Columbus Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

Equipment age plays a big role. With regular service, central air typically lasts 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like higher energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement is cheaper and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of summer.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Consider a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and just like that a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
